What the bounds tell you

Mean and median intervals describe unresolved search, not statistical confidence. The uncertainty from sampling different shuffled dungeons is separate.

A reproducible study

One objective

Maximize final score. Surviving scores equal remaining health; a final potion at full health adds its value. Defeat scores include negative health after the fatal blow and all unresolved monsters.

One information model

The oracle knows the initial dungeon order and how avoiding a room changes it. There is no residual randomness within a fixed deal, so an action’s EV equals its eventual score under the best continuation.

Verified lower bounds

Room-based beam search finds complete legal lines. Memoized exhaustive search improves them. Each result is replayed through the rules engine. An admissible upper bound assumes all remaining healing can be used and ignores future damage.

Strict weapon chains

A weapon’s next monster must be strictly weaker than its previous kill. Potions are limited to one effective use per room; avoiding consecutive rooms is prohibited. The final room is fully cleared.

Seeds, search budget, and sources

Every row uses its own seeded Fisher–Yates shuffle of the 44-card deck. Seeds are a reproducible pseudorandom sample, not an enumeration of all 44! orders. The reported bounds apply to this sample, not the entire population of possible deals.

Rules: Zach Gage & Kurt Bieg’s original Scoundrel, also available in this readable mirror. Inspiration: the video tutorial. The original PDF’s wording about equal monster ranks is ambiguous; this study uses strictly decreasing weapon chains.
