Octaves without the guesswork

Keep the left thumb in a gap between rollers. The gap between the two textured rollers is register 0; move toward the mouthpiece to go up. This guide uses the manual’s relative positions, −2 through +4.

Same note, next octave

Keep the note keys; move the thumb one register up. G4 → G5 needs no front-key changes.

Crossing C♯ → D

The open C♯ fingering sits at the top of a register. Main D uses the next register up. An alternate high D or low C♯ can avoid this thumb move.

Use the planner to compare thumb moves. A transposed patch can change the sounding notes.

What the other controls do

Breath sensor

Air pressure shapes level and tone. Tongue gently to separate notes; keep air steady for connected phrases.

Mouthpiece / bite

Gentle bite pressure adds vibrato or pitch expression. Avoid hard biting; the mouthpiece contains a sensor.

Ground plates

Rest the right thumb on the neutral grounding area; keep the left thumb on the rollers while playing.

Pitch-bend plates

Right-thumb contact with the upper or lower plate bends pitch up or down. Stay centered for unbent notes.

Glide plate

Left-thumb contact activates portamento between pitches. Avoid brushing it when practicing clean octave changes.

Hold

Sustains a note or sends an assigned control message, depending on the setting. Turn it off for plain melody practice.

Octave / Interval

The 4000s Octave and 5000 Interval buttons add a second voice or an assigned function. They are separate from the thumb rollers.

Transpose

Shifts the sounding pitch while the fingering stays the same. Turn it off when matching this chart.

Program & setup

Program chooses the sound; Setup changes instrument behavior. On the 5000, Hold increases values and Interval decreases them.

FX & sensor knobs

FX selects effect settings. The covered adjustment knobs set breath, glide, bend and vibrato response; use the manual’s calibration procedure.

Button assignments and sensor settings depend on model and firmware. The linked manuals cover sound editing, connections, calibration, effects and MIDI.

Start playing

  1. Connect and hear it.

    Power the 4000s using batteries or its specified adapter. Use PHONES for headphones or LINE OUT for an amp/interface. MIDI OUT sends performance messages, not audio; a computer needs a MIDI interface.

  2. Choose standard EWI mode.

    On the 4000s/5000, use Setup → fingering (Fn) → EWI (Ei). The diagrams here do not apply to sax, flute, oboe, EVI or reversed EVI mode.

  3. Support the instrument.

    Use the neck strap. Keep hands relaxed, fingers close to the sensors and thumbs in playing contact.

  4. Learn one chromatic set.

    Start with the main fingerings. Add alternatives when a particular transition is awkward.

  5. Practice the move slowly.

    Loop two notes before a whole phrase. Coordinate the thumb and fingers; then increase speed.

If something sounds wrong

Check fingering mode, transpose, accidental bend/glide contact, and Hold/Interval first. If unintended notes remain, consult your model’s touch sensitivity and note-delay settings. More note delay can hide uneven finger changes but adds latency.
