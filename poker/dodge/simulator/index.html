      Rules & method

      The game

      • Two to four players are each dealt one card from a 52-card deck.
      • The player holding the lowest card draws first. Ties are broken by suit: clubs are lowest, then diamonds, hearts, spades. By default the ace counts as the highest card for this rule; untick the option to count it low.
      • Players draw one card at a time in clockwise seat order and keep every card.
      • A player busts when their cards contain a straight or better (straight, flush, full house, four of a kind, straight flush, royal flush). Aces play high or low in straights, no wraparound. A busted player draws no more cards.
      • The last player still drawing wins. If the deck runs out first, the survivors split the pot equally.

      Statistics

      • Equity is the chance to win the pot, from a Monte Carlo simulation of the whole table from the current position (splits count fractionally).
      • Bust next / in 2 / in 3 are exact: every one-, two- and three-card completion from the remaining deck is enumerated.
      • Draws to bust counts a player's own further draws including the bust card, as if they alone kept drawing from the current deck. The first three draws are exact and the tail is simulated.
      • Seventeen cards always contain five of a suit, so a hand of n cards busts within 17 − n draws. With four players the deck can run out: three players at 17, 17 and 9 cards leave a fourth player with only 9 cards and no deck.

      Doubling cube

      • Money-game cube, as in backgammon. It starts centered at 1. Before drawing, the player on turn may double. The opponent then takes (the cube doubles and they own it; only the owner may redouble) or passes (the doubler wins the current stake).
      • The solver expands every possible next card exactly. Each resulting position is valued by a dynamic program over the cube states on a race model that uses each player's hazard sequence (probability of busting on each future draw given survival), with the first draws exact and the tail simulated. Deep precision adds a second exact ply.
      • Shown equities are in points at the current cube value and as the equivalent win chance ((E + 1) / 2 at a cube of 1). The correct double decision compares No double against the smaller of Double/Take and Double/Pass; the correct response takes when Double/Take beats −1 cube.
      • In this game the take point is the dead-cube 25%: two-ply analysis moves take equities by less than 0.005, because games usually end by a bust before the cube owner can redouble. Doubles come early (65% win chance on average) since one card can end the game.
