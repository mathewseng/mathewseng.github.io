    How the solver works & sources

    For conventional five-card poker, the solver compares all 32 holds for each of the 2,598,960 starting hands (2,869,685 with a joker). It sums payouts over all legal draws from the 47 unseen cards (48 with a joker). Discarded cards cannot return. For single stake, the average best return is RTP. Double on draw adds 31 decisions (every hold except standing pat), for 63 total. The solver maximizes net profit after the full stake: a 1× draw returning r has net EV r − 1; a 2× draw has net EV 2r − 2. RTP is expected payout divided by expected total stake. Single stake is preferred on ties, which are reported separately.

    Subset sums and inclusion–exclusion make this exhaustive calculation fast enough to run locally. For the five-card poker variants, these are complete enumerations using floating-point arithmetic, not Monte Carlo estimates. Changing payouts or qualification rules recalculates strategy. Max-coin royal payouts are normalized to a one-credit bet.

    Classic paytables checked against Wizard of Odds. Solver source, tests & reproducible analysis.

    Cribbage has 16, 32, 64 or 128 holds, plus every non-pat hold at 2× when enabled. Its exact hand solver groups draws by rank multiplicity, then counts suit assignments with generating functions and inclusion–exclusion. This preserves interactions between runs, flushes, suited jacks and the payout floor without a huge seven-card table. Cribbage displays expected net PnL per initial hand, including the fee and all stakes. Four- and five-card game EVs are exhaustive. Six- and seven-card game EVs use reproducible sampled initial deals with exact strategy for each deal and are marked as estimates. Edited six- and seven-card configurations can be estimated locally from 128 deals.

    The library covers conventional single-draw games. Multi-stage multiplier games such as Ultimate X and specialty side bets use different rules and are outside this solver.

    One draw. Your choice.

    Bet one practice credit, receive the game’s four to seven cards, and hold any combination. Draw replaces every unheld card from the unseen deck. Your final hand receives the listed return. The base bet is always 1. Session PnL starts at zero and updates only after each completed hand, adding the payout minus the full stake, including the extra stake at 2×. Practice play can continue with negative PnL; there is no bankroll to refill.

    Classic & bonus games

    Use standard poker rankings. Aces can be high or low in straights. Bonus games split four-of-a-kind payouts by rank and sometimes by the fifth card, called the kicker. Only one category pays.

    All payouts include the stake. A 1× return is a push. Royal flushes use the conventional max-coin rate of 800×, normalized to this game’s one-credit wager.

    Wild cards

    Deuces Wild uses all four twos as wild cards. Joker Poker adds one fully wild joker to a 53-card deck. Wild cards can make five of a kind. Natural royals, wild royals, and four deuces have separate awards. A natural hand contains no wild card.

    Double on draw

    Every custom game appears in the game menu with and without 2× on a draw. Each version keeps its own payouts and qualification settings. After seeing the deal and choosing holds, use Double & draw to add one credit and double the entire final payout. You must replace at least one card. Standing pat stays at one credit. Qualifying in Badeucey still pushes: a doubled qualifying hand returns both credits, plus twice its points.

    Doubling a particular draw is better than taking that same draw at single stake only if its expected one-credit return exceeds 1. The solver also compares every other hold, including standing pat. With doubling enabled, decision EVs show net profit after all stakes. Game RTP uses total stakes; net EV uses one initial deal. Practice play always allows the extra stake when you draw at least one card.

    Cribbage · 4, 5, 6 or 7 cards

    The Cribbage menu contains 16 games: each size has Capped loss and Points − fee versions, both with and without optional 2× on draw. Score all final cards with no starter: each combination totaling 15 scores 2, each pair scores 2, every longest run of at least 3 scores its length (including multiplicities), a flush of at least 4 scores its length, and each jack with another card of its suit scores 1. Aces are low and count 1; T, J, Q and K count 10 toward fifteens.

    In Capped loss, return = max(0, points − fee), including your stake. The maximum loss is 1, or 2 at 2×. With fee 10, scores of 10 or less lose, 11 pushes, and 14 wins +3. At 2×, that win is +6. The fee is an editable integer. Full-length runs and flushes extend the OFC rules to six and seven cards. In Points − fee, net PnL = points − fee: zero points loses the fee, matching the fee pushes, and exceeding it wins the difference. The maximum loss is the fee, or twice the fee at 2×. Each version saves its own fee and scoring settings. The editor also offers the original OFC scoring cap of 5.

    Every cribbage hold has an exact draw EV. Cribbage EV is expected net PnL per initial hand, after the fee and all stakes. Four- and five-card game EVs enumerate all initial deals. Six- and seven-card game EVs sample initial deals and solve all decisions exactly for each. These estimates do not guarantee that a fee stays below zero EV.

    2–7 Low

    Aces are always high. Pairs, straights, and flushes lose. The best hand is 7-5-4-3-2, the wheel. A-2-3-4-5 is ace-high, not a low straight. Each hand pays its single best category: the wheel pays 10×, not 10× plus 7-high.

    Your original table pays J-high 1×, T-high 2×, 9-high 3×, 8-high 4×, 7-high 5×, and the wheel 10×. These are total returns, including the stake.

    Badeucey

    By default, both qualifiers are required: five distinct ranks with no straight or flush, and a four-card badugi (four different ranks and four different suits). Aces are high in both halves; the best badugi is 5-4-3-2.

    Qualifying returns your 1-credit stake. Add the best low award and the best badugi award. Awards within each half do not stack. Qualifying with neither point award is a push. Failing an active qualifier loses the bet. T-high low and T-high four-card badugi awards are editable integer points, initially 0.

    In the payout lab, choose high card only, unpaired hands (including straights and flushes), or any five-card hand. Set the minimum badugi size to four, three, two, or none, and optionally cap ranks in the five-card hand and qualifying badugi. A qualifying badugi subset must meet the minimum size and rank limit.

    Relaxed qualification changes who gets the push. Five-card low points still require an unpaired, non-straight, non-flush 2–7 low, and badugi points still require four different ranks in four suits. The awards are scored independently after all active qualifiers are met. A three-card badugi can qualify and earn low points, but receives no four-card badugi points.

    For example, 7♠ 5♥ 4♦ 3♣ 2♥ has a wheel and a 7-high badugi: 1 + 8 + 4 = 13 credits returned. All custom games use one draw.
