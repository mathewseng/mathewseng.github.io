    Return per 1-unit stake

    0 turns a category off

    The highest paying matching category wins. Returns include the stake; categories do not stack. Straights use distinct consecutive faces, up to six.

    These are editable practice presets. Rerolling changes the odds; the presets are not calibrated to a fair return.

    One roll. One draw.

    Roll 2–7 fair six-sided dice. Tap a die to hold it, then reroll the rest once. You can change holds before drawing, or keep the whole hand. A rerolled die can show the same number again.

    Dice cribbage

    All three scoring groups add together. Each physical die is distinct, even when faces match.

    Pairs · 1 point each
    Every pair of matching dice counts: a pair scores 1, trips 3, quads 6, five alike 10, six alike 15, and seven alike 21.
    Sums of six · 1 point each
    Every nonempty combination totaling six counts. A lone 6 scores 1. Two 3s score 1 for the sum and 1 for the pair.
    Runs · 3 or more consecutive faces
    By default, only the longest runs score, once per combination. 1–2–3–4 scores 4 run points. 1–2–2–3 has two runs of three for 6, plus a pair for 1 and two sums of six for 2: 9 total. A run cannot repeat a face or wrap from 6 to 1. Runs top out at six; a seventh die can multiply a run.

    The alternative “Every run” rule also counts shorter runs inside longer ones. With that setting, 1–2–3–4 scores 10 run points.

    Choose how points pay

    Capped loss: return = max(0, points − fee), including the 1-unit stake. Net PnL = return − 1. With fee 10, 10 points loses 1, 11 pushes, and 14 wins 3.

    Points − fee: net PnL = points − fee. With fee 10, 0 points loses 10, 10 pushes, and 14 wins 4.

    Double & reroll: choose after seeing the first roll. It multiplies the entire final gain or loss by two, including the fee or stake. Capped loss can lose 2; points − fee can lose twice the fee. Keeping all dice cannot be doubled.

    Dice poker

    Use the editable category paytable. Only the highest paying qualifying category pays, including when a shorter straight pays more than a longer one. Patterns can be subsets of the hand; a full house needs three of one face and two of another. Straights of 3, 4, 5, and 6 are available when you have enough dice. Set a return to 0 to disable it.

    Yahtzee-style

    A single-hand variation with one reroll, not a full scorecard game. Take the best available score: Chance (sum of all dice), three or four alike (sum), full house (25), straights of 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 (20 / 30 / 40 / 50), or 5 / 6 / 7 alike (50 / 75 / 100). Apply either points payout mode and your chosen fee.

    Exact hold advice

    The solver checks every hold against every possible replacement roll, weighted by its probability. It maximizes net PnL with the current fee, payouts, and doubling rule. It includes keeping all dice, and prefers a single stake on a tie. Advice is for this hand; it does not claim the whole preset is fair.
