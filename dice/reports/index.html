How the math works

Each mode partitions all 6N ordered rolls into mutually exclusive outcome categories. Hand ranks follow the displayed order. Four-dice full sets rank trips before two pair. Six-dice full sets share payouts for trips / boat and three pair / quads.

Chosen face: w(N,k) = C(N,k) × 5N−k. Single sets: group rolls by their largest matching set. Full sets: distinguish the complete multiplicity pattern, ignoring the identities of the faces. Sums: add all pips; exact counts come from repeatedly convolving the six equally likely die faces.

For set modes, each face-frequency vector contributes N! / ∏ count[face]! ordered rolls. Add those integer counts within each category; their sum is always 6N.

P(category j) = w[j] / 6N
EV = Σ w[j]p[j] / 6N

A fair schedule has Σ w[j]p[j] = 0. With L categories, solve p[L−1] = −Σj<L−1 w[j]p[j] / w[L−1]. The final weight is 1 for a chosen face or sum and 6 for set modes. Every set-mode weight is divisible by 6, so reducing the weights restores a unit final weight. Integer bounds prune impossible branches.

Sums use a catalogue of readable rules rather than the general enumeration above. Ranges may share payouts; bonuses and multipliers can interrupt the progression. Each proposed rule is checked with the same exact integer EV identity. See About ranking for the sum-specific score and search coverage.

Fairness uses exact integer arithmetic, never rounded probabilities. Search bounds keep every Number operation within JavaScript’s safe integer range; the checker and EV proofs use BigInt. Percentages, volatility, charts, and style scores are display approximations.

Standard deviation measures payout volatility per roll, in net units: σ = √(Σ P[j](p[j]−EV)²). Variance is σ², in units². These are weighted by the exact outcome probabilities, including grouped hands. For a fair schedule EV = 0, so σ = √(Σ w[j]p[j]² / 6N). Lower means smaller swings; it does not change fairness.

Amount simplicity: use absolute values. Zero scores 100. Preferred amounts, in order, are 1, 2, 3, 5, 4, 10, 6, 8, 12, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 75, 100; their scores run from 98 down to 64 in steps of 2. Other amounts score 70/(1 + factor cost). Prime-factor costs are 2 → .05, 3 → .075, 5 → .09, and prime q > 5 → .09 + log₂(q/5). Repeated factors count again, plus .025 for each factor after the first.

Base schedule simplicity = 75% mean amount simplicity + 25% shared-factor score. Amounts count equally across payout categories, or distinct payout values in Sums. The shared score is the percentage of nonzero payouts divisible by the most widely shared prime, requiring at least two payouts. Ties use the smallest prime. Zeros are simple pushes but do not count toward sharing; ±1 count as nonzero amounts without factors. For −1 / 2 / 4 / 6 / 8, four of five share 2, so the shared-factor score is 80. Whole-schedule scaled copies are still excluded.

Prime payout penalty: for every payout whose absolute value q is itself a prime above 5, multiply base simplicity by 7/(4q). One ±7 multiplies it by 0.25; one ±11 by about 0.159. Multiple prime payouts compound. Composites such as 14 and 49 pay the prime-factor costs above, but do not incur this extra prime-payout penalty.

Unlisted amount penalty: each nonzero payout whose absolute value is outside the preferred list multiplies both Simplicity and Recommended by 0.1. One unlisted amount removes 90% of each score; two remove 99%. Zero is exempt. Sums count each distinct payout value once. Preferred-only schedules rank far above these in Recommended and Simplest; other ranking methods still use their chosen metric.

Smoothness = 100 / (1 + σ(d) / mean(|d|)), where d is the sequence of adjacent jumps. Higher means more even jumps. Constant payouts score 100.

Steepness = 100 × Σ max(d[k],0)(k−1)/(L−2) / Σ max(d[k],0). Equal positive jumps score 50; a final-only rise scores 100. With just two categories, a positive jump scores 100. No rises score 0. L is the number of categories.

Recommended (hand modes) = (.50 × S + .30 × starting-loss preference + .10 × smoothness + 5/(1 + max/50) + 2.5/(1 + bend) + 2.5 × increasing-jump share) × U. S is simplicity after prime penalties but before the unlisted amount penalty; U = 0.1 to the power of the number of unlisted payouts. The unlisted penalty is applied once to each score. Starting-loss preference is 300/(r + 3), where r is its zero-based position in the allowed order: −1 scores 100, −2 scores 75, −3 scores 60. Unlisted starting losses score zero and are excluded from generated schedules. Bend averages |log₂((|next jump|+1)/(|previous jump|+1))−1|, plus 1 for each shrinking jump. The maximum term uses max(0, maximum payout).

Winning means a positive net payout. The highest-ranked category has probability 1/6N for a chosen face and 6/6N for set modes, even if a non-increasing schedule pays its maximum elsewhere.

    Score out of 100: 50 for simplicity (prime factors and shared factors), 30 for the starting-loss preference, 10 for smooth jumps, 5 for a moderate maximum, 2.5 for jumps growing around twofold, and 2.5 for increasing payouts. Then multiply the entire score by 0.1 for each nonzero payout whose absolute value is outside the preferred list.

    Recommended keeps the best score in each curve family: same initial payout, jackpot power-of-two band, and normalized intermediate payouts in eighths. Other rankings show individual schedules.

Parts are the number of base ranges, or 3 for a periodic/stair-step rule. A range bonus or multiplier adds 0.5 cost. Every distinct unlisted payout multiplies both Simplicity and the entire score by 0.1. This favors repeated ranges and short rules.

    Std dev is the probability-weighted standard deviation of net payout per roll. Lower means smaller swings. Click its header to sort the shortlist, or use Rank by to search for the lowest or highest.

    This search is finite because of the allowed starting losses and payout bounds. If you remove the bounds and ordering rules, the general problem can have infinitely many distinct fair integer schedules: for two dice with a chosen face, (−1, −m, 25+10m) is primitive and fair for every integer m ≥ 1. The two-category set game is an exception: with a negative initial payout, its only nonzero fair proportion is −1 / +5.

    How the comparison works

    We compare total PnL with independent sessions using the same sequence of payout tables and number of rolls. Percentile is the chance of a lower PnL plus half the chance of a tie. Higher means you have done better.

    Session variance adds each roll’s probability-weighted variance. Standard deviation is its square root; for one unchanged table, σ = per-roll σ × √rolls. The bands are centered on expected PnL, which is zero for these fair games.

    Band probabilities come from the payout distribution, so they need not match 68%, 95%, and 99.7%. For exceptionally large distributions beyond the calculation budget, the panel explicitly labels a normal estimate; standard deviation still uses the actual dice odds.

    Reset PnL starts a new comparison. These statistics describe the session so far, not the next roll.
