How the math works

Each mode partitions all 6N ordered rolls into mutually exclusive outcome categories. Hand ranks follow the displayed order. Four-dice full sets rank trips before two pair. Six-dice full sets share payouts for trips / boat and three pair / quads.

Chosen face: w(N,k) = C(N,k) × 5N−k. Single sets: group rolls by their largest matching set. Full sets: distinguish the complete multiplicity pattern, ignoring the identities of the faces.

For set modes, each face-frequency vector contributes N! / ∏ count[face]! ordered rolls. Add those integer counts within each category; their sum is always 6N.

P(category j) = w[j] / 6N
EV = Σ w[j]p[j] / 6N

A fair schedule has Σ w[j]p[j] = 0. With L categories, solve p[L−1] = −Σj<L−1 w[j]p[j] / w[L−1]. The final weight is 1 for a chosen face and 6 for set modes. Every set-mode weight is divisible by 6, so reducing the weights restores a unit final weight. Integer bounds prune impossible branches.

Fairness uses exact integer arithmetic, never rounded probabilities. Search bounds keep every Number operation within JavaScript’s safe integer range; the checker and EV proofs use BigInt. Percentages, volatility, charts, and style scores are display approximations.

Standard deviation measures payout volatility per roll, in net units: σ = √(Σ P[j](p[j]−EV)²). Variance is σ², in units². These are weighted by the exact outcome probabilities, including grouped hands. For a fair schedule EV = 0, so σ = √(Σ w[j]p[j]² / 6N). Lower means smaller swings; it does not change fairness.

Amount simplicity: ignore the sign. Zero scores 100, one scores 98. Otherwise factor the amount into primes, counting repeats. Factor costs are 2 → .05, 3 → .075, 5 → .09, and a prime q > 5 → .09 + log₂(q/5). Add .025 for each factor after the first. The score is 100/(1 + total cost). This gives 0 > 1 > 2 > 3 > 5 > 4; large primes and repeated factors reduce simplicity.

Base schedule simplicity = 75% mean amount simplicity + 25% shared-factor score. Amounts count equally across payout categories. The shared score is the percentage of nonzero payouts divisible by the most widely shared prime, requiring at least two payouts. Ties use the smallest prime. Zeros are simple pushes but do not count toward sharing; ±1 count as nonzero amounts without factors. For −1 / 2 / 4 / 6 / 8, four of five share 2, so the shared-factor score is 80. Whole-schedule scaled copies are still excluded.

Prime payout penalty: for every payout whose absolute value q is itself a prime above 5, multiply base simplicity by 7/(4q). One ±7 multiplies it by 0.25; one ±11 by about 0.159. Multiple prime payouts compound. Composites such as 14 and 49 pay the prime-factor costs above, but do not incur this extra prime-payout penalty.

Smoothness = 100 / (1 + σ(d) / mean(|d|)), where d is the sequence of adjacent jumps. Higher means more even jumps. Constant payouts score 100.

Steepness = 100 × Σ max(d[k],0)(k−1)/(L−2) / Σ max(d[k],0). Equal positive jumps score 50; a final-only rise scores 100. With just two categories, a positive jump scores 100. No rises score 0. L is the number of categories.

Recommended = .50 × simplicity + .20 × smoothness + 10/(1 + max/50) + 10/(1 + bend) + 10 × increasing-jump share. Bend averages |log₂((|next jump|+1)/(|previous jump|+1))−1|, plus 1 for each shrinking jump. The maximum term uses max(0, maximum payout).

Winning means a positive net payout. The highest-ranked category has probability 1/6N for a chosen face and 6/6N for set modes, even if a non-increasing schedule pays its maximum elsewhere.

    WHY RECOMMENDED?

    Simple amounts, balanced growth, and a moderate jackpot. Similar curves are grouped so more shapes surface.

    Scoring & search limits

    Score out of 100: 50 for simplicity (prime factors and shared factors), 20 for smooth jumps, 10 for a moderate maximum, 10 for jumps growing around twofold, and 10 for increasing payouts.

    Recommended keeps the best score in each curve family: same initial payout, jackpot power-of-two band, and normalized intermediate payouts in eighths. Other rankings show individual schedules.

    Search stops at 600,000 candidate nodes or about 2.5 seconds. Partial results are ranked only among the schedules explored, not the entire space. Starting losses and payout intervals are interleaved for coverage.

    Ranking & coverage

    Complete means every integer schedule inside the current bounds was considered, then scaled copies were removed. Partial means the search hit 600,000 candidate nodes or about 2.5 seconds; results are the best of the explored candidates only. Narrow the bounds for a complete search.

    The table is a shortlist, limited by Maximum results. Recommended also keeps one representative per similar curve family. Other ranking methods do not group curves. A complete search does not mean every result is displayed.

    Recommended score: 50% simplicity, 20% smoothness, 10% moderate maximum, 10% progressive jumps, and 10% increasing payouts. Exact formulas are in “How the math works.” Rank is the shortlist position for the selected ranking method; header sorting preserves that rank.

    Simplicity: 0 > 1 > 2 > 3 > 5 > 4. Large prime factors cost more; repeated small factors have a mild cost. Examples of amount scores: 0 = 100, 1 = 98, 2 ≈ 95.2, 3 ≈ 93.0, 5 ≈ 91.7, 4 ≈ 88.9, 7 ≈ 63.5, 11 ≈ 44.9. Schedule simplicity combines 75% average amount score with 25% shared-factor score: the percentage of nonzero payouts sharing the most common prime factor (at least two must share it). Negative signs are ignored. Then each payout that is itself a prime q > 5 multiplies the score by 7/(4q): ±7 cuts it to one quarter, and larger primes cost even more. Multiple prime payouts compound. The inspector shows this deduction.

    Std dev is the probability-weighted standard deviation of net payout per roll. Lower means smaller swings. Click its header to sort the shortlist, or use Rank by to search for the lowest or highest.

    Without bounds, there can be infinitely many distinct integer payout proportions, even after removing scaled copies. For two dice with a chosen face, (−m, 1, 25m−10) is fair and has no common divisor for every integer m ≥ 1. The two-category set game is an exception: with a negative initial payout, its only nonzero fair proportion is −1 / +5.
