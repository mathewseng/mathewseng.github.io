# Backgammon Vision

The [player](https://mathewseng.github.io/backgammon/vision/) shows the five
Office-3 game replays from November 17, 2026. Add `?game=1` through `?game=5`
to link to a specific game.

The 1080p video files are assets on the
[Office-3 release](https://github.com/mathewseng/mathewseng.github.io/releases/tag/office3-backgammon-20261117-v15).
The published site contains the viewer and stills from the replays. The media
loads over the network on playback. **Vision** toggles a frame-aligned overlay
over the same video element, preserving playback, time, and seeking. The
compressed audit sidecars are generated by `scripts/v4/export_audit.py`. They
contain the board-only frame-level detections for the selected game. **Analysis**
shows the reconstructed position and the five highest-scoring moves. Rolls
stay visible through supported hand occlusions. The recent actions area keeps
completed checker moves and cube decisions readable when play moves on.
Every supported cube opportunity has an EV comparison, including a roll
without doubling. Checker and cube loss are tracked in separate columns.
Best and played choices have separate labels. The review board uses the
colors of the recorded table.
The active roll shows equity after best play until a confirmed result. Once
play ends, the overlay clears checker counts and shows the final loss totals.
The review includes a separate playable row when the recorded move ranked
outside the top five.
[Review notes](REVIEW.md) record the footage and timing checks for this update.

Use **Previous action** and **Next action** or the Left and Right Arrow keys
to jump between rolls and cube offers. Timeline marks are colored by player.
Cube marks carry the offered face. The speed menu supports 1× through 10×.
Use **Review** beside the video to inspect the current decision. The seekable history below
the video opens the same analysis view as Backgammon Play. **Download history** exports a
Backgammon Library backup. Import it through **Library → Import**. A gap in the board sequence starts a new replay segment. The
backup includes available board snapshots for unresolved rolls and retains
timestamped observations in its replay index. A physically continuous roll
appears once even when the detector drops and rediscovers it.

The **Reports** tab below the player opens a downloadable audit for the selected
game. It shows detector coverage, unresolved faces, model benchmark errors,
pickup and turn-order checks, and reviewed corrections. The timestamp buttons
seek the replay. A coverage figure describes the evidence available to the
model. A graded play's legality is checked separately from its visual agreement.

**Frame data** downloads the reconstructed state for the selected video. Number
frames from zero and find the last `changes` entry whose `frame` is at or before
the desired frame. Resolve its `position` and `roll` references in the same file.
Position points run from 1 to 24 and use positive counts for White and negative
counts for Black. Bar, borne-off counts, and losses are in White, Black order.
`movingPoints` supplies the in-turn point counts when they differ from the last
settled board. Each roll also records its own fixed `before`, confirmed
`after`, and `resultFrame` so a later checker movement cannot change the
position used for that decision. Missing boards are null. A null roll means
there is no active roll in the reconstructed timeline.
A null die face means the other face could not be established. An unknown cube
owner stays explicit even when the face or stake is readable. Use `sources` to
locate the original camera clip and frame offset.
The `ending` entry marks a reviewed reset or pack-up. Game 5’s inferred pass
is marked in both `result` and `cubeOffer`.

The [vision pipeline](scripts/README.txt) contains the source used to reconstruct
the game state, score the decisions with GNUbg, and render the overlays. Site
assembly excludes this research code. The local footage, model weights and
generated game state used by the pipeline live in the artifact directory
described in the pipeline README.
