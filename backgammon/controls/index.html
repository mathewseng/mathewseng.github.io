What happens when I click?

Tap a disc to select it. Tap the open point area to play there.

Follow the questions from top to bottom. Only your own checkers with a legal move can be selected. During a preview or the opponent’s turn, the board does not accept moves.

  1. 1

    Did you click your checker disc?

    Yes →

    Already selected? Deselect it.

    Otherwise, if it has a legal move, select it and show its destinations.

    If that resident checker cannot move, a highlighted destination underneath still works. Otherwise, keep your selection.

    No selectable disc · continue ↓
  2. 2

    Can the selected checker reach this point?

    Yes →

    Play the selected checker.
    This also works onto a friendly stack. A highlighted return or dice-revision target revises the draft.

    No · if selected, switch to a movable resident or clear selection. With no selection, continue ↓
  3. 3

    Can a checker move or change dice to land here?

    Yes →

    Play the nearest legal checker.
    Repeated clicks in the open point area can build a point or use doubles. If no forward move lands here, use a legal dice revision—even after both dice were used. An active selection limits this to its own routes.

    No · continue ↓
  4. 4

    Is this a legal draft-return target?

    Yes →

    Return the nearest eligible checker.
    Return it to its pre-roll origin and restore its dice, even if that point holds checkers. Intermediate stops are not orange targets; use Undo for a single step. A checker whose journey includes a forced prefix cannot return past it.

    No · continue ↓
  5. 5

    Is a movable checker on this point?

    Yes →

    Select that checker.
    If already selected, deselect it.

    No legal action → clear selection without a message

Why is this highlighted?

Highlights show legal actions, never the engine’s preferred move. Turn off “Show legal-move hints” in Settings → Display & controls to keep only the selected checker marked. The same click rules still apply.

They use complete legal turns with the unused dice, including combined moves and doubles. Bar entry, blocked points, maximum dice use and bearing off all apply. Colors follow your board theme; rings, arrows and labels also identify the action.

  1. 1

    Can you move on this board right now?

    No →

    No legal-destination highlights.
    Opponent turns, previews and non-moving phases do not offer moves. Last-turn markers and keyboard focus have separate meanings.

    Yes · continue ↓
  2. 2

    Is a checker selected?

    Yes →

    Only that checker’s routes are highlighted.
    Its disc has a solid selection ring. Other source rings disappear. Check its legal forward moves, dice-revision landings and draft returns below.

    No · use all eligible checkers ↓
  3. 3

    No selection: can this checker move or return?

    Yes →

    Show a dashed source ring.
    A checker with a legal remaining-dice move, dice revision or original-position return is a source. Blocked checkers have no source ring. Only the bar is a forward source while entry is required.

    For each destination · continue ↓
  4. 4

    Can an eligible checker reach this location?

    Yes →

    Show a colored point wash and outline.
    With a selection, show the dice used and a landing ring where space permits. A sum means one checker uses multiple dice; ↔ revises its journey using its own dice and any unused dice. Both bar/24/20 and bar/21/20 with 1–4 offer 21 and 24. Other checkers’ moves and compulsory steps stay fixed. Legal revisions glow before selection too. Legal bearoff destinations light the OFF tray.

    Also check for a return ↓
  5. 5

    Does this location restore a draft move?

    Yes →

    Use the undo color and one clean outline.
    Amber in the default theme; ↶ when selected. A selected return takes visual priority over a forward route. Without selection, the forward highlight wins if both are possible. Only the checker’s pre-roll origin is a return target; intermediate and forced stops are excluded.

    No forward or return route → no destination highlight

Last move: outlined checkers mark where the opponent ended; hollow ghosts show where they started. These are history, not available moves.

Keyboard focus: a rectangular outline shows which point receives keyboard input. Focus does not mean the point is legal or selected.

Every interaction case

A ring describes a source; a point wash describes a destination. Click a movable disc to select it. Click open point space to play the destination. With a selection, ordinary clicks respect its displayed routes.

These cases cover the behavioral combinations, not an enumeration of every backgammon position. Only routes that obey the complete-turn rules exist. Colors follow your theme; labels, ring styles and arrows keep their meaning. Read overlapping cases in the flowchart’s priority order.

No double-click mode

Fast and slow clicks follow the same rules. Open-point taps can build a point while the arrival remains legal; checker taps select. A hidden timing rule made that distinction unpredictable, especially after automatic selection. Shift+point keys make destination intent explicit without needing fast fingers.

Unavailable means quiet

If a location has no action, clear the selected checker without moving anything or showing a message. Blank board and page space do the same. Buttons, usable dice and other controls keep their usual actions. Historical ghosts and focus outlines do not create actions. Invalid drops simply snap back.

When there is a real choice

Equivalent routes need no prompt. A combined shortcut prefers a hitting route; different hits or dice use may open a chooser. Move through an intermediate point manually to avoid a hit.

Bar, undo & dragging

Enter from the bar first. Legal routes respect all dice and bearing-off rules. Drag to a highlighted destination; an invalid drop cancels. Select a moved checker and its dashed return target, or use Undo/Reset.

Keyboard

Point keys select or play the selected route. Press the selected point again to deselect. Shift+point explicitly requests destination input, including building a point. There is no timing requirement. Ordinary keys retain the unambiguous undo-only shortcut. Change bindings in Settings.
