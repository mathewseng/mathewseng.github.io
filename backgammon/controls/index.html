Advanced · the default

Click a checker for one die, or a destination for a direct or combined move. Settings → Display lets you switch to Beginner.

Advanced hides source rings and destination hints. Ghosts and arrows describe past moves, not legal options. Dragging and the legal-step selector in Details remain available. Press C or use the large Confirm control to submit; Z undoes a draft step and X resets the editable draft.

Beginner · what happens when I click?

Tap a disc to select it. Tap the open point area to play there.

Follow the questions from top to bottom. Only your own checkers with a legal move can be selected. During a preview or the opponent’s turn, the board does not accept moves.

Why is this highlighted?

Highlights show legal actions, never the engine’s preferred move. Turn off “Show legal-move hints” in Settings → Display & controls to keep only the selected checker marked. The same click rules still apply.

They use complete legal turns with the unused dice, including combined moves and doubles. Bar entry, blocked points, maximum dice use and bearing off all apply. Colors follow your board theme; rings, arrows and labels also identify the action.

Last move: outlined checkers mark where the opponent ended; hollow ghosts show where they started. These are history, not available moves.

Keyboard focus: a rectangular outline shows which point receives keyboard input. Focus does not mean the point is legal or selected.

Every interaction case

A ring describes a source; a point wash describes a destination. Click a movable disc to select it. Click open point space to play the destination. With a selection, ordinary clicks respect its displayed routes.

These cases cover the behavioral combinations, not an enumeration of every backgammon position. Only routes that obey the complete-turn rules exist. Colors follow your theme; labels, ring styles and arrows keep their meaning. Read overlapping cases in the flowchart’s priority order.

More input rules

No double-click mode

Fast and slow clicks follow the same rules. Open-point taps can build a point while the arrival remains legal; checker taps select. A hidden timing rule made that distinction unpredictable, especially after automatic selection. Shift+point keys make destination intent explicit without needing fast fingers.

Unavailable means quiet

If a location has no action, clear the selected checker without moving anything or showing a message. Blank board and page space do the same. Buttons, usable dice and other controls keep their usual actions. Historical ghosts and focus outlines do not create actions. Invalid drops simply snap back.

When there is a real choice

Equivalent routes need no prompt. A combined shortcut prefers a hitting route; different hits or dice use may open a chooser. Move through an intermediate point manually to avoid a hit.

Bar, undo & dragging

Enter from the bar first. Legal routes respect all dice and bearing-off rules. Drag a checker to a destination; an off-target release snaps to its closest legal destination. Escape cancels. In Advanced mode, an amber checker with a return symbol can be clicked to undo: it has no forward move and its draft is editable. Used dice also undo; desktop Z undoes and X resets the editable draft.

Keyboard

Point keys select or play the selected route. Press the selected point again to deselect. Shift+point explicitly requests destination input, including building a point. There is no timing requirement. Ordinary keys retain the unambiguous undo-only shortcut. Change bindings in Settings.
